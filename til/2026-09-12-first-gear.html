Pipeline Attacks Expose Energy Corridors

Aggregation

World

Critical / Energy Security Iraq says Saudi pipeline attacks came from its territory

Pre-read: Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline matters because it lets crude move from eastern oil fields to the Red Sea without transiting the Strait of Hormuz. When that route is hit, oil-market risk shifts from a single chokepoint story to a wider infrastructure-defense problem.

Summary: AP reports that Iraq said recent drone attacks on the Saudi pipeline originated from Iraqi territory, prompting an investigation, a commander dismissal in Maysan province, and temporary Saudi pipeline shutdowns. The attacks pushed oil above $100 and landed alongside intensified regional pressure from Houthis, Iran-linked actors, and disrupted Red Sea shipping. The operational lesson is blunt: energy security now depends on defending long inland logistics corridors, not only ports and maritime chokepoints.

Critical / AI Security Anthropic says threat actors used Claude for weapons, spying and repression

Pre-read: Frontier labs are becoming private intelligence collectors because abuse shows up first in account telemetry, tool-call traces, and model-interaction patterns. That makes safety reporting a live national-security interface, not a compliance footnote.

Summary: Anthropic's new misuse report says actors tied to Russia, China, Yemen, Iran and commercial surveillance operations tried to use Claude for military engineering, espionage, surveillance, influence work, cyber operations and model distillation. Business Insider's read highlights Russian drone-swarm work, a China-linked anti-torpedo proposal, Yemen-based missile guidance attempts, and Iran-linked maritime intelligence workflows. The durable signal is that model providers now see operational evidence of hostile AI workflows before many governments or buyers do, which raises the value of shared misuse intelligence and the risk of every lab sitting on a partial picture.
