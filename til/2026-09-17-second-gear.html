Critical / Robotics Safety How close to that robot are you willing to get?

Pre-read: Industrial humanoid safety is running into a certification shape mismatch: an August 2026 arXiv paper argues that legged robots often need an actively controlled safe state because simply cutting power can create a fall hazard. That makes safety evidence a live software-and-controls problem, not just a hardwired stop button problem.

Summary: Andra Keay interviews Polymath Robotics CTO Ilia Baranov about robot near-misses, protective stops, and the new Open Source Safety Consortium. The useful distinction is concrete: an emergency stop may remove power, while a protective stop should drive the robot toward a defined safe state, such as slowing a vehicle, lowering a crane load, or keeping a humanoid balanced. The related protective-stop repository shows the project is already more than a manifesto: it includes hardware, firmware, ROS 2 support, host logic, tests, and safety-case documentation, while explicitly warning that OSHWA certification is not functional-safety certification. For robotics founders, the wedge is continuous safety evidence around deployed autonomy, teleoperation, networked stop commands, and operating-domain drift.
