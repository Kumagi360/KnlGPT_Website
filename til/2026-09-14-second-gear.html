ICE Arrests Outrun Deportations

World

Critical / Government Operations ICE arrests keep soaring. Here's why they are not leading to more deportations

Pre-read: Immigration enforcement is a throughput system, not just an arrest count: detention space, hearing schedules, removal orders, foreign-government acceptance, transportation, and litigation all determine whether an arrest becomes a deportation. Reuters is useful here because it follows the operational chain instead of treating enforcement pressure as a single policy lever.

Summary: Reuters reports that ICE arrests have climbed sharply, but preliminary U.S. government data it reviewed shows deportations have not risen proportionally. The newsletter excerpt says the gap reflects legal and logistical limits, including a growing share of people arrested without criminal convictions, charges, or existing deportation orders. The important model update is bureaucratic: more surveillance, data sharing, and local cooperation can increase intake faster than courts, detention capacity, and removal processes can clear cases.

Critical / Neurotechnology The Expanding Potential of Neuromodulation

Pre-read: Neuromodulation has crossed from lab technique into clinical infrastructure: deep brain stimulation, transcranial magnetic stimulation, vagus nerve stimulation, and responsive neurostimulation now sit inside regulated care pathways rather than speculative neurotech decks. The bottleneck is shifting toward measurement, targeting, and closed-loop control, which makes this a useful systems read for anyone thinking about adaptive hardware in messy human environments.

Summary: Contrary argues that neuromodulation's next frontier is precision, especially in psychiatry, where treatment often still depends on fixed stimulation settings and periodic clinical assessment. The report contrasts open-loop devices with adaptive systems such as responsive stimulation, then explains why psychiatric disorders are harder than movement disorders: heterogeneous symptoms, weak biomarkers, circuit-level ambiguity, and poor longitudinal measurement. The founder-relevant point is the missing data layer between device capability and personalized therapy: clinics need better ways to map circuits, track response, and adjust stimulation before adaptive neuromodulation can scale beyond narrow indications.
