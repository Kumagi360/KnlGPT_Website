Ideation

Idea 1 Deployment Provenance for Modular Structures

Sell a compliance and evidence system for companies that finance, insure, ship, lease, or install modular structures in politically sensitive regions. The new primitive is not supply-chain provenance for parts; it is deployment provenance for fast physical facts: where a structure actually appears, whether it matches permits or sanctions rules, who touched it, and what evidence would survive a regulator, lender, or court asking later.

Why Now

Modular buildings, mobile energy systems, pop-up telecom, disaster shelters, and autonomous construction equipment make physical deployment faster than permitting and sanctions review. At the same time, governments are expanding entity, territory, service, and import restrictions, so a bank or insurer can no longer treat location as a static address field.

First Wedge

Start with insurers and trade-finance teams covering modular construction, temporary housing, telecom shelters, and mobile power units near sanctioned, disputed, or disaster-recovery zones. Give them a pre-bind and ongoing monitoring file: geofenced risk checks, permit evidence, satellite-change alerts, counterparty links, and an audit packet for each asset.

Commercial Model

The buyer is the risk, compliance, or underwriting owner who already pays for sanctions screening, geospatial data, legal review, and site audits. Price per monitored asset or financed project, with premium tiers for satellite tasking, human verification, and regulator-ready evidence packs.

Defensibility

The product compounds through asset fingerprints, deployment histories, permit/outpost/land-record mappings, and reviewer decisions that improve future routing. Planet, BlackSky, insurers, and compliance vendors each own pieces; the defensible wedge is the workflow graph that connects a specific modular asset, its documents, its counterparties, its physical location, and the decision trail.

Technical Risk

The hard part is not seeing buildings from space; it is reliably matching a financed or insured asset to a changing physical object with partial paperwork, adversarial counterparties, clouds, lookalike structures, and weak land records. False positives create customer pain, while false negatives create legal exposure.

Market Expansion

After modular housing, expand into mobile substations, temporary cell towers, containerized compute, mining camps, border infrastructure, and autonomous construction fleets. The same primitive applies anywhere cheap physical deployment creates liability before the back office catches up.

Self-Critique

This could be too narrow if sanctions pressure stays symbolic or if buyers decide occasional manual legal review is cheaper than continuous monitoring. It also risks becoming a services-heavy investigations shop unless the first customers have enough repeat assets to train reusable fingerprints and routing rules.

Next Experiment

In two weeks, interview 10 trade-finance, political-risk insurance, and modular-construction compliance buyers. Bring a demo built from public satellite imagery, sanctions lists, permit records, and three known disputed-zone deployments; test whether they would pay for an audit packet before binding, lending, or renewing coverage.
