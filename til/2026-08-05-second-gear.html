Happenings Michigan tests Democrats' left turn

Pre-read: Michigan is a Senate-control state, so a primary there is also a test of how much ideological risk Democrats can carry into a competitive general election. The primary shows progressive energy; November will test whether that energy is additive in the places Senate majorities are decided.

Critical / AI Systems How Big Models Teach Small Models to Be Smart

Pre-read: Distillation is moving from model-compression trick to production architecture: large models can serve as teachers while small models handle cheap, low-latency, narrow jobs. Hinton, Vinyals, and Dean framed the core idea in 2015 as transferring a cumbersome model's knowledge into a more deployable one via soft output distributions. The useful question now is where the teacher-student boundary gives you capability without inheriting too much hidden behavior.

Summary: ByteByteGo explains distillation as training a separate student model to imitate a larger teacher, rather than pruning or quantizing the teacher itself. The piece walks through soft labels, synthetic data generation, fine-tuning, evaluation, and automated loops where the teacher creates data, scores the student, and iterates. It is clearest on the operating constraints: narrow tasks benefit most, teacher quality sets much of the ceiling, too-wide capacity gaps can hurt, and architecture can matter more than raw parameter count.

The stronger adjacent signal is that this is becoming a workflow, not only a technique. Google said Gemma 3 used distillation to improve lightweight open models, while a 2026 agentic distillation paper reports a closed loop for SMS-threat detection where teacher choice strongly affected outcomes. The risk side is equally concrete: a Nature paper on subliminal learning reports that behavioral traits can transfer through semantically unrelated generated data. Distillation is becoming a small-model factory, and provenance has to follow the factory floor.

Ideation

Ideation

Idea 1 Distillation Residue Lab

Sell regulated AI teams a test gate for small models trained from larger teachers. Before a bank, insurer, hospital vendor, or device maker ships a distilled model, the product builds a teacher bill of materials, runs task-specific residue assays, compares alternate teacher lineages, and issues a deployment certificate that says what the student appears to have inherited and where it is unsafe to use.

Why Now

Frontier models are becoming cheap teachers, and small models are becoming the default way to put AI inside phones, call centers, factories, clinics, and private clouds. At the same time, provenance is no longer just paperwork: if generated training data can carry hidden teacher behavior, a derivative model needs a lineage and residue check before it touches regulated workflows.

First Wedge

Start with regulated customer-support and fraud-review teams distilling frontier models into small classifiers or routing agents. The first product is a CI step plus expert report: compare two or three teacher choices, run adversarial and bias residue suites, flag inherited refusal or risk-taking patterns, and produce evidence for model risk management.

Commercial Model

The buyer is the head of AI platform, model risk, or security at a regulated enterprise. Charge per certified model lineage and annual platform access, with higher pricing for production monitoring and regulator-ready evidence packs. Budget exists because these teams already pay for model validation, red-teaming, audit support, and vendor-risk reviews.

Defensibility

The compounding asset is a private corpus of teacher-student fingerprints: which teacher families leak which behavioral residues into which student architectures on which tasks. Generic lineage tools can say where a model came from; this company gets better at predicting what a student will inherit and which teacher produces the safer narrow model for a given workflow.

Technical Risk

The hard part is not storing provenance. It is building assays that catch non-semantic inherited behavior without claiming impossible guarantees. Version one should be honest: bounded task suites, teacher A/B comparisons, canary prompts, representation-level similarity checks where available, and post-deployment drift monitors tied to the original certificate.

Market Expansion

After support and fraud classifiers, expand to on-device medical triage, insurance claims, industrial safety copilots, malware and phishing filters, and autonomous workflow routers. The morphology is the same: a larger teacher produces or labels data, a smaller student runs cheaply in a constrained environment, and the buyer needs proof that the derivative model did not inherit unacceptable behavior.

Self-Critique

Generic AI governance and provenance are crowded, and incumbents can add lineage metadata. The wedge only works if residue testing becomes a painful production blocker, not a nice-to-have audit artifact. It could also be too early if enterprises keep using hosted frontier models instead of producing many small derivatives.

Next Experiment

In four weeks, pick one narrow task such as complaint triage or SMS fraud detection. Distill the same small model from three different teachers, run a public bias, refusal, and risk-appetite assay plus task accuracy, and show a model-risk leader a one-page certificate that changes which teacher they would approve.
