Critical / Real Estate Post-Pandemic, 'Living at the Office' Takes on New (and Lucrative) Meaning

Pre-read: Remote work has settled into a durable real-estate shock: WFH Research has kept U.S. work-from-home share far above the pre-pandemic baseline, while Moody's data reported by Axios put U.S. office vacancy at a record 21% in Q1 2026. That turns obsolete office space into a collateral, tax, and urban-design problem before it becomes a housing solution.

Summary: The Daily Upside reports that office-to-residential conversions are finally arriving at visible scale after years of zoning, permitting, and construction work, with Chicago's Loop completing its first conversion and New York conversions accounting for a large share of new housing this year. The constraint is physical and financial selection: plumbing, HVAC, floor plates, elevators, stairs, light, air, acquisition basis, tax incentives, and permitting shrink the viable inventory. The broader supply impact remains limited; McKinsey estimated that converting all excess office space would increase housing stock in superstar cities by less than 3%, and the NYC Comptroller found a 44-building pipeline could produce about 17,400 apartments while depending heavily on policy design and tax tradeoffs.
