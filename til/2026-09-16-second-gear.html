Critical / Campaign Finance Deep in Trump country, a revolt against corporate money could reshape political spending

Pre-read: Citizens United pushed campaign-finance fights toward disclosure, super PACs, and state-level workarounds; Montana is testing a different lever by regulating corporate powers granted under state law. Ballotpedia lists I-194 as a 2026 initiative on excluding political spending from artificial persons' charter powers, while Public Citizen says corporate-backed super PAC spending has already topped $500 million this cycle.

Summary: Reuters reports that Montana voters will decide whether to make the state the first in the country to bar corporations from election spending, with similar efforts underway in more than 30 states. The article frames the measure as a legal workaround to Citizens United: it targets the scope of state-granted corporate powers rather than directly challenging corporate speech rights. The stakes are practical as well as constitutional; Reuters cites OpenSecrets data showing known corporate contributions to federal outside political groups rising from $13 million in 2010 to more than $600 million so far this year. This is worth opening because the story shows how anti-dark-money politics is moving from federal doctrine into state corporate law.

Critical / Retrieval How LLMs Can Find a Needle in a Haystack

Pre-read: Enterprise agents fail in boring, expensive ways when retrieval treats policies as generic text instead of governed records. Recent RAG evaluation work keeps finding the same hard surface: answer quality depends on retrieval, filtering, and citation discipline before the model starts writing.

Summary: ByteByteGo gives a clean systems walk-through of the retrieval stack behind useful LLM answers: chunk boundaries, embedding compatibility, similarity metrics, flat search versus IVF and HNSW, metadata filters, version changes, hybrid search, reranking, and no-answer handling. The sharpest founder wedge is policy freshness rather than vector storage: regulated agents need to know which source is eligible, which version is active, and when a confident answer should be refused. Read it as a compact map of where enterprise RAG still breaks under real-world document change.
